Most career advice optimises for the next move. The next title, the next
raise, the next logo on the CV. It treats a career as a series of jumps.

That framing costs people more than they realise.

## The compounding view

A career that compounds is one where each year of work makes the next year
easier, not just better paid. Three things do the compounding:

- **Skills that transfer.** Judgement, writing, and reading a room outlast
  any specific tool or employer.
- **Relationships that persist.** The people who vouch for you are built
  slowly and spent quickly.
- **A body of visible work.** Things you made that others can point at.

> The question is not "what is my next role?" It is "what am I building
> that will still be worth something in ten years?"

## Where to start

Pick one of the three. Give it a standing hour each week. Review it in
ninety days.

What would you add to the list?
